# 11 — Comparison with AvaFrame

The MLS-MPM solver against AvaFrame's com1DFA benchmarks, com4FlowPy and weBIGeo's earlier
trajectory model. All four run on the same five real avalanches, on AvaFrame's own DEMs, and are
rated with AvaFrame's own evaluation method (AIMEC, Fischer 2013). The tools and the procedure
are in [avaframe/README.md](avaframe/README.md); this file holds the setup, the results and
what they do and do not show.

## 1. Setup

**Test cases.** The five real datasets of AvaFrame 2.1 that come with com1DFA null-test
benchmarks (no entrainment, no resistance):

| dataset | avalanche | release | volume | com1DFA footprint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| avaKot | Kontertal | relKot | 21 681 m³ | 11.7 ha |
| avaGar | Gallreide | relGar | 61 828 m³ | 28.2 ha |
| avaHit | Hintertux | relHit | 267 918 m³ | 120.8 ha |
| avaWog | Wolfsgruben | relWog | 165 292 m³ | 54.3 ha |
| avaMal | Madlein | relMal1to3 | 438 846 m³ | 133.3 ha |

**Sandbox.** The MPM runs directly on AvaFrame's DEM, in metres and on its 5 m grid, with
AvaFrame's release area and slope-normal thickness (phases 1–2). The map's terrain plays no part,
so differences come from the models, not from terrain or coordinates.

**Models.**
- **com1DFA** (reference): AvaFrame's benchmark runs, v1.11, samosAT friction (samosATSmall for
  avaKot), ρ = 200 kg/m³, stopped below 1 % of peak kinetic energy. Taken from AvaFrame,
  not rerun.
- **com4FlowPy** (D'Amboise et al. 2022): run through AvaFrame 2.1 with its defaults (α 25°,
  exponent 8, flux threshold 3·10⁻⁴), as in the earlier weBIGeo evaluation.
- **weBIGeo trajectories** (Komon et al. 2025, `ComputeAvalancheTrajectoriesNode`): on the
  sandbox DEM, with the earlier evaluation's parameters (α 25°, deviation 24°, persistence 0.4,
  2 m steps, 8 paths per release cell).
- **MPM**, 65 536 particles, cell size max(DEM cell, domain/512), 12 grid layers, stopped like
  com1DFA (at most 400 s). Three materials × two basal laws:
  - materials: Stomakhin 2013 (film-snow preset), Drucker–Prager (cohesionless, φ 13.3°, from
    Li's M = 0.5), Cohesive Cam Clay with Li et al. 2021's case V parameters (back-calculated
    from the Vallée de la Sionne avalanche of 2003);
  - basal laws: Coulomb with the preset's μ (0.47, or 0.49 for Li V), and Voellmy with
    com1DFA's Voellmy parameters μ 0.155, ξ 4000 m/s².

**Material choice.** The result depends on the snow material, so the choice needs stating.
These are the app's existing presets, one per material model, none tuned to these avalanches:

| material model | preset | E / ν | ρ | parameters | Coulomb μ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stomakhin 2013 | film snow | 0.14 MPa / 0.2 | 400 | ξ 10, θc 2.5·10⁻², θs 7.5·10⁻³ | 0.47 |
| Drucker–Prager | cold dense, cohesionless | 3 MPa / 0.3 | 250 | φ 13.3° (from Li's M = 0.5) | 0.47 |
| Cohesive Cam Clay | Li 2021 case V (Vallée de la Sionne 2003) | 3 MPa / 0.3 | 200 | M 0.7, β 0.2, ξ 0.002, p₀ 3 kPa | 0.49 |

Why these:
- **Li case V** is the only preset back-calculated from a real avalanche, and it uses the
  benchmarks' density of 200 kg/m³. It is the solver's default for that reason.
- **Drucker–Prager** is the cohesionless counterpart, with φ derived from Li's case I M = 0.5.
- **Stomakhin** is the project's original material, made for film visuals, as the baseline.
- **Friction.** Coulomb is what Li uses on real terrain. Voellmy with com1DFA's μ 0.155 and
  ξ 4000 is the closest we have to com1DFA's law; there is no samosAT in the MPM.
- **No per-avalanche tuning.** com1DFA's benchmarks also use one parameter set for all cases.
  Calibrating per avalanche would make the MPM fit com1DFA by construction.

Consequences:
- **Li's cases I–IV and a μ sweep were run afterwards** (findings 1–3). The ranking changed:
  Li I (cold dense) fits best.
- **The material behaviour is only partly resolved at 5–7.7 m cells.** Li uses 0.5 m.
- **None of the presets is known to match the events' snow.**

**Rating.** AIMEC compares each result with the com1DFA reference in the coordinates of the
avalanche path (AvaFrame's `path_aimec`), with each benchmark's own AIMEC settings (domain
width, start of the runout area). Two thresholds:
- **ppr > 1 kPa**, AvaFrame's default runout criterion;
- **pft > 0.1 m**, the flow thickness. It ignores the MPM's sub-centimetre layer of single
  particles. For com4FlowPy and the trajectories pft is a footprint flag, so this variant
  compares footprints.

Reported per run:
- runout difference (MPM minus com1DFA, along the path);
- runout (travel) angle, against com1DFA's;
- recall and precision of the affected area in the runout area;
- footprint;
- peak velocity;
- simulated time;
- computing time.

## 2. Results

Generated by `avaframe/summarise.py` from the runs of `avaframe/batch_phase6.json`
(2026-10-10, Apple M5). One row per run is in `avaframe/results/phase6.csv`, and AIMEC's own
plots are in each run's `aimec/` folder.

How to read the tables:
- **"≥".** The result is still above the threshold in the last cross section of AvaFrame's
  AIMEC path, so its runout is a lower bound. com1DFA never reaches the path end. A run marked
  "≥" also has a runout angle that is too steep.
- **"walls".** The share of particle-substeps at the domain walls. The MPM domain is the whole
  DEM (512 cells of 5.0–7.7 m), and walls stay at 0–0.1 %.
- **com4FlowPy** took 11–146 min per dataset on all CPU cores. avaGar and avaHit ran alongside the
  MPM batch and AIMEC, so their wall times include contention.
- **Footprint** is pft > 0.1 m for the MPM and cells reached for com4FlowPy and the trajectories.

## 3. What the results show

All figures use the pft > 0.1 m variant; per-avalanche values are in the tables above. The
detailed report, with app screenshots of every run (the 2D sandbox view and the map), is an HTML
page built by `avaframe/report.py`.

1. **Closest to com1DFA: Cam Clay with Li 2021's cold dense parameters (case I) and Coulomb μ 0.47.**
   - Mean runout difference 103 m, travel angle 1.4° off on average.
   - Always slightly too long: +189 m (avaKot), +128 (avaGar), +77 (avaHit), +30 (avaWog),
     +93 (avaMal).
   - It reaches 94–100 % of com1DFA's runout area on avaKot, avaGar and avaHit, 77 % on avaWog,
     and only 24 % on avaMal, where it ends in a different part of the fan. Precision is 0.26–0.80.
2. **The material matters more than the friction.**
   - Li's cohesive regimes stop far too early: case II warm shear (mean 355 m), III sliding slab
     (380 m), IV warm plug (551 m). On Wolfsgruben and Madlein they never reach the runout area.
   - Stomakhin's film snow behaves the same way (368 m).
   - Li V, new snow from the Vallée de la Sionne back-calculation, lies in between: +155 m to
     −279 m, mean 140 m.
3. **One constant Coulomb μ cannot fit all five avalanches.** For Li V, the mean runout
   difference is 141 m at μ 0.40, 111 m at 0.47, 140 m at the preset 0.49 and 175 m at 0.55. The
   per-avalanche optimum differs:
   - the avalanches with com1DFA's steeper travel angles (28.2–31.0°) fit best at μ 0.55:
     avaKot +77 m, avaGar +19, avaHit +29;
   - the two with the flattest (25.5° and 25.4°) fit best at μ 0.40: avaWog +44 m, avaMal +67 m.

   Under Coulomb friction a flow keeps roughly the same travel angle everywhere, set by μ and the
   material's dissipation. com1DFA's travel angles vary by 5.6° across the five avalanches, because
   its samosAT friction depends on flow depth and speed. So no single μ can match all five.

   This is not simply release size: avaHit has the second-largest release (268 000 m³) and still
   fits at μ 0.55. Making the friction depend on flow depth and speed (Voellmy, samosAT) is the way to
   capture it.
4. **The speeds are 2–2.5× com1DFA's.** The variants that reach com1DFA's runout reach 97–139 m/s,
   against com1DFA's 40–53 m/s. The slow variants (Li II–IV and Stomakhin, 40–75 m/s) are the ones
   that stop short. No variant gets both right with Coulomb friction. Pressure is ρv², so it is
   off by a factor of 4–6 where the runout matches.
5. **Voellmy with com1DFA's parameters (μ 0.155, ξ 4000) runs too far in every material.**
   - Mean 294–326 m.
   - For each material, three of the five runs pass the end of the AIMEC path, so their
     runouts are lower bounds.
   - Travel angles 21–25° against com1DFA's 25–31°.
   - Speeds stay at 75–127 m/s, so the drag barely acts.

   The parameters are calibrated for com1DFA's depth-averaged equations. In the MPM the drag
   g|v|²/(ξh) uses the column's B-spline depth and acts only on nodes below the terrain. Whether
   that h is too large is **open**, and the next thing to check in the solver.
6. **Drucker–Prager (cohesionless, φ 13.3°) with Coulomb** is always too long, by +103 to
   +247 m (mean 192 m, 2.7°), and among the fastest variants (101–132 m/s).
7. **weBIGeo's earlier trajectory model**, with the earlier evaluation's parameters, does well on
   the long, flat-ending avalanches: avaWog +44 m, avaMal −22 m. It overshoots the steep, short
   ones: avaKot +575 m, avaGar ≥ +297 m. With a fixed α of 25°, an energy-line model can only
   match avalanches whose travel angle is near 25°; com1DFA's are 25.4–31.0°. Its footprints
   are 1.1–7× com1DFA's.
8. **com4FlowPy** (same α 25°) shows the same pattern, but milder: +474 m (avaKot), +200 (avaGar),
   +96 (avaHit), −44 (avaWog), −115 (avaMal). Its mean, 186 m and 2.5°, lies between the
   trajectory model and the best MPM variants.
9. **Computing time.**

   | model | time | for |
   |---|---|---|
   | MPM | 2–73 s GPU, 3–124 s wall with rendering | 49–368 s simulated, faster than real time on an M5 |
   | trajectory model | about 1 s | one run |
   | com4FlowPy | 11–146 min, on the CPU | one dataset |
   | com1DFA | 118–174 s | the benchmark's simulated run time, not its computing time, which the metadata does not give |

   So these data allow no fair comparison of computing time with com1DFA.

**Overall.** In AvaFrame's own terms, the MPM reproduces com1DFA's runout to about 100 m and its
travel angle to about 1.5° on average, with a fluid-like material (Li I cold dense, or Li V at
μ 0.47) and plain Coulomb friction. That is better than both data-driven runout models
(com4FlowPy 186 m and 2.5°, trajectory model 229 m and 3.4°), at faster than real time on a laptop
GPU.

It does not reproduce the speeds, and therefore not the pressures. A single Coulomb μ also cannot
match avalanches whose travel angles differ, and com1DFA's Voellmy parameters do not carry over.
Both point to the basal friction law as the next thing to work on, not the snow material.

## 4. Limits of this comparison

- **No samosAT friction in the MPM.** com1DFA's benchmark friction law has no counterpart here;
  Voellmy with com1DFA's own Voellmy parameters is the closest.
- **Resolution.** MPM cells of 5–6.4 m against Li et al.'s 0.5 m. A 1 m release is a fifth of a
  cell thick, so the material regimes Li describes cannot develop at this resolution. The
  material presets carry Li's parameters, not Li's behaviour.
- **One reference.** com1DFA is a model, not an observation. Agreement with it is not
  agreement with nature; avaGar, avaWog and avaMal have documented events (AvaFrame's
  shapefiles), but no observed runouts are part of the data used here.
- **Thresholds and conversions.** The MPM's pft is column depth × cos(slope), its pfv the mean
  particle speed per column. com4FlowPy's and the trajectory model's pfv come from the
  energy-line height. ppr = ρ pfv² uses each run's density (MPM presets 200–400 kg/m³), so the
  ppr variant is not density-neutral; the pft variant is.
- **The AIMEC path is finite.** Runouts beyond its end show as "≥" and count with their lower
  bound in the means. The models that run furthest therefore look somewhat better than they are.
- **One run per configuration.** The solver is deterministic, so a rerun gives the same result.
  Material (Li I–V) and basal friction (μ 0.40–0.55) were varied; particle count and cell size
  were not.
- **Computing time.** Wall time includes rendering and was measured while com4FlowPy ran on the
  CPU. GPU time is the solver's own, from timestamp queries (Apple M5). com1DFA's run time is
  from the benchmark metadata, on unknown hardware.
