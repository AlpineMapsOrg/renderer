# 11 — Comparison with AvaFrame

The MLS-MPM solver against AvaFrame's com1DFA benchmarks, com4FlowPy and weBIGeo's earlier
trajectory model. All four run on the same five real avalanches, on AvaFrame's own DEMs, and are
rated with AvaFrame's own evaluation method (AIMEC, Fischer 2013). The tools and the procedure
are in [avaframe/README.md](avaframe/README.md); this file holds the setup, the results and
what they do and do not show.

## 1. Setup

**Test cases.** The five real datasets of AvaFrame 2.1 that come with com1DFA null-test
benchmarks (no entrainment, no resistance):

| dataset | avalanche | release | volume | com1DFA footprint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| avaKot | Kontertal | relKot | 21 681 m³ | 11.7 ha |
| avaGar | Gallreide | relGar | 61 828 m³ | 28.2 ha |
| avaHit | Hintertux | relHit | 267 918 m³ | 120.8 ha |
| avaWog | Wolfsgruben | relWog | 165 292 m³ | 54.3 ha |
| avaMal | Madlein | relMal1to3 | 438 846 m³ | 133.3 ha |

**Sandbox.** The MPM runs directly on AvaFrame's DEM, in metres and on its 5 m grid, with
AvaFrame's release area and slope-normal thickness (phases 1–2). The map's terrain plays no part,
so differences come from the models, not from terrain or coordinates.

**Models.**
- **com1DFA** (reference): AvaFrame's benchmark runs, v1.11, samosAT friction (samosATSmall for
  avaKot), ρ = 200 kg/m³, stopped below 1 % of peak kinetic energy. Taken from AvaFrame,
  not rerun.
- **com4FlowPy** (D'Amboise et al. 2022): run through AvaFrame 2.1 with its defaults (α 25°,
  exponent 8, flux threshold 3·10⁻⁴), as in the earlier weBIGeo evaluation.
- **weBIGeo trajectories** (Komon et al. 2025, `ComputeAvalancheTrajectoriesNode`): on the
  sandbox DEM, with the earlier evaluation's parameters (α 25°, deviation 24°, persistence 0.4,
  2 m steps, 8 paths per release cell).
- **MPM**, 65 536 particles, cell size max(DEM cell, domain/512), 12 grid layers, stopped like
  com1DFA (at most 400 s). Three materials × two basal laws:
  - materials: Stomakhin 2013 (film-snow preset), Drucker–Prager (cohesionless, φ 13.3°, from
    Li's M = 0.5), Cohesive Cam Clay with Li et al. 2021's case V parameters (back-calculated
    from the Vallée de la Sionne avalanche of 2003);
  - basal laws: Coulomb with the preset's μ (0.47, or 0.49 for Li V), and Voellmy with
    com1DFA's Voellmy parameters μ 0.155, ξ 4000 m/s².

**Rating.** AIMEC compares each result with the com1DFA reference in the coordinates of the
avalanche path (AvaFrame's `path_aimec`), with each benchmark's own AIMEC settings (domain
width, start of the runout area). Two thresholds:
- **ppr > 1 kPa**, AvaFrame's default runout criterion;
- **pft > 0.1 m**, the flow thickness. It ignores the MPM's sub-centimetre layer of single
  particles. For com4FlowPy and the trajectories pft is a footprint flag, so this variant
  compares footprints.

Reported per run:
- runout difference (MPM minus com1DFA, along the path);
- runout (travel) angle, against com1DFA's;
- recall and precision of the affected area in the runout area;
- footprint;
- peak velocity;
- simulated time;
- computing time.

## 2. Results

Generated by `avaframe/summarise.py` from the runs of `avaframe/batch_phase6.json`
(2026-10-10, Apple M5). One row per run is in `avaframe/results/phase6.csv`, and AIMEC's own
plots are in each run's `aimec/` folder.

How to read the tables:
- **"≥".** The result is still above the threshold in the last cross section of AvaFrame's
  AIMEC path, so its runout is a lower bound. com1DFA never reaches the path end. A run marked
  "≥" also has a runout angle that is too steep.
- **"walls".** The share of particle-substeps at the domain walls. The MPM domain is the whole
  DEM (512 cells of 5.0–7.7 m), and walls stay at 0–0.1 %.
- **com4FlowPy** took 11–146 min per dataset on all CPU cores. avaGar and avaHit ran alongside the
  MPM batch and AIMEC, so their wall times include contention.
- **Footprint** is pft > 0.1 m for the MPM and cells reached for com4FlowPy and the trajectories.

## 3. What the results show

1. **Closest to com1DFA: Cohesive Cam Clay with Li 2021 case V parameters and Coulomb μ 0.49.**
   - Mean runout difference 135 m, runout angle 1.5° off on average (0.7–2.4°), no run cut off
     at the path end.
   - The spread: +164 m (avaKot), +87 (avaGar), +67 (avaHit), −54 (avaWog), −303 (avaMal).
   - On avaMal it stops short, so its runout-area recall is only 0.02 there.
   - Where it reaches the runout area it covers 0.95–1.00 of com1DFA's footprint, but spreads
     wider: precision 0.35–0.52.
2. **The speeds are 2–2.5× com1DFA's.** All Coulomb variants except Stomakhin reach 92–134 m/s
   against com1DFA's 40–53 m/s. Coulomb friction has no velocity-dependent resistance; samosAT
   does. That Li V still stops near com1DFA's runout means the rest of the energy goes into the
   material's plastic flow; the energy line's μ_eff is above μ. A model that gets the runout and
   not the speed is not yet a hazard model: pressure is ρv².
3. **Voellmy with com1DFA's parameters (μ 0.155, ξ 4000) runs too far in every material.**
   - Mean runout difference 266–326 m, up to ≥ +598 m on avaKot.
   - Up to three of five runs reach the end of the path.
   - Runout angles 21.0–25.4°, against com1DFA's 25.4–31.0°.
   - Speeds stay at 76–128 m/s: the drag barely acts.

   The parameters are calibrated for com1DFA's depth-averaged equations. In the MPM the drag
   g|v|²/(ξh) uses the column's B-spline depth and acts only on nodes below the terrain. Whether
   that h is too large there is **open**, and the next thing to check in the solver.
4. **The Stomakhin 2013 film-snow material with Coulomb** has the most realistic speeds (45–57 m/s)
   and hits avaKot's runout (+8 m). On the long avalanches it stops early: −1000 m on avaWog and
   −775 m on avaMal, never reaching the runout area. Its elastic, compaction-hardening snow
   behaves like a sliding block. It was made for film visuals, and this shows it.
5. **Drucker–Prager (cohesionless, φ 13.3°) with Coulomb** comes second on runout (mean 178 m,
   2.6°), always too long, and with the highest speeds (92–131 m/s).
6. **weBIGeo's earlier trajectory model**, with the earlier evaluation's parameters, is good on
   the long, flat-ending avalanches: avaWog +44 m, avaMal −22 m. It overshoots the steep, short
   ones: avaKot +575 m, avaGar ≥ +297 m. With a fixed α of 25°, an energy-line model can only
   match avalanches whose runout angle is near 25°; com1DFA's are 25.4–31.0°. Its footprints
   are 1.1–7× com1DFA's.
7. **com4FlowPy** (same α 25°) shows the same pattern, but milder. It is too long on the steep
   ones (+474 m avaKot, +200 m avaGar), close on avaHit (+96 m), and short on the long ones
   (−44 m avaWog, −115 m avaMal). Mean 186 m and 2.5°, between the trajectory model and the
   best MPM variant.
8. **Computing time.**

   | model | time | for |
   |---|---|---|
   | MPM | 3–67 s GPU, 5–103 s wall with rendering | 51–231 s simulated, faster than real time on an M5 |
   | trajectory model | about 1 s | one run |
   | com4FlowPy | 11–146 min, on the CPU | one dataset |
   | com1DFA | 118–174 s | the benchmark's simulated run time, not its computing time, which the metadata does not give |

   So there is no fair comparison of computing time with com1DFA from these data.

**Overall.** In AvaFrame's own terms, the MPM can reproduce com1DFA's runout to about 100–300 m
and its travel angle to about 2°, with the right material (Cam Clay with Li V parameters) and plain
Coulomb friction. It does not reproduce the speeds and pressures, and com1DFA's Voellmy
parameters do not carry over. As a physical model of the flow it is still behind the
depth-averaged reference. As an interactive model that runs in a browser-class GPU budget
faster than real time, it already beats the data-driven runout models on runout and travel angle
(Cam Clay: 135 m and 1.5°; com4FlowPy: 186 m and 2.5°; trajectory model: 229 m and 3.4°).

## 4. Limits of this comparison

- **No samosAT friction in the MPM.** com1DFA's benchmark friction law has no counterpart here;
  Voellmy with com1DFA's own Voellmy parameters is the closest.
- **Resolution.** MPM cells of 5–6.4 m against Li et al.'s 0.5 m. A 1 m release is a fifth of a
  cell thick, so the material regimes Li describes cannot develop at this resolution. The
  material presets carry Li's parameters, not Li's behaviour.
- **One reference.** com1DFA is a model, not an observation. Agreement with it is not
  agreement with nature; avaGar, avaWog and avaMal have documented events (AvaFrame's
  shapefiles), but no observed runouts are part of the data used here.
- **Thresholds and conversions.** The MPM's pft is column depth × cos(slope), its pfv the mean
  particle speed per column. com4FlowPy's and the trajectory model's pfv come from the
  energy-line height. ppr = ρ pfv² uses each run's density (MPM presets 200–400 kg/m³), so the
  ppr variant is not density-neutral; the pft variant is.
- **The AIMEC path is finite.** Runouts beyond its end show as "≥" and count with their lower
  bound in the means. The models that run furthest therefore look somewhat better than they are.
- **One run per configuration.** The solver is deterministic, so a rerun gives the same result.
  There is no sensitivity study (particles, cell size, layers) yet.
- **Computing time.** Wall time includes rendering and was measured while com4FlowPy ran on the
  CPU. GPU time is the solver's own, from timestamp queries (Apple M5). com1DFA's run time is
  from the benchmark metadata, on unknown hardware.
