# References

Numbering matches the Notion proposal so the report can reuse it.

These notes cite and paraphrase; they don't reproduce text from the papers. Pull actual
wording from the sources.

**[1]** J. U. Brackbill and H. M. Ruppel. 1986. *FLIP: A method for adaptively zoned,
particle-in-cell calculations of fluid flows in two dimensions.* Journal of Computational
Physics 65 (1986), 314–343.
→ Origin of the hybrid particle/grid scheme. Background only; nothing implemented directly.

**[2]** D. Sulsky, Z. Chen, and H. L. Schreyer. 1994. *A particle method for
history-dependent materials.* Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Eng. 118, 1-2 (1994), 179–196.
→ MPM proper: extends FLIP to solid mechanics, material points carry deformation state.
Source of the four-stage loop.

**[3]** Yuanming Hu, Yu Fang, Ziheng Ge, Ziyin Qu, Yixin Zhu, Andre Pradhana, and Chenfanfu
Jiang. 2018. *A moving least squares material point method with displacement discontinuity
and two-way rigid body coupling.* ACM Trans. Graph. 37, 4, Article 150 (Aug. 2018), 14 pages.
→ **The formulation implemented.** MLS replaces the B-spline gradient; force and APIC affine
momentum collapse into one matrix applied to the node offset. Used in `mpm_p2g` / `mpm_g2p`
(the `4/dx²` and `4/dx` factors come from here). Also the source for the claim that grid
transfers dominate MPM cost.

**[4]** P. Lancaster and K. Salkauskas. 1981. *Surfaces generated by moving least squares
methods.* Mathematics of Computation 37, 155 (1981), 141–158.
→ The MLS approximation underlying [3]. Cited for the method, not used directly.

**[5]** Alexey Stomakhin, Craig Schroeder, Lawrence Chai, Joseph Teran, and Andrew Selle.
2013. *A material point method for snow simulation.* ACM Trans. Graph. 32, 4, Article 102
(July 2013), 10 pages.
→ **The constitutive model implemented.** Fixed-corotated elasticity, singular-value
clamping for plasticity, exponential compaction hardening. Table 1 supplies every default
material parameter in the code. Implemented in `snow_stress()` / `apply_plasticity()`.

**[6]** Michael Liu, Xinlei Wang, and Minchen Li. 2025. *CK-MPM: A Compact-Kernel Material
Point Method.* ACM Trans. Graph. 44, 4, Article 152 (August 2025), 14 pages.
→ **Not implemented** — stretch goal. Reduces cell-crossing instability with two shifted
grids; combines with MLS-MPM or plain MPM. Would touch only the transfer kernels and grid
allocation.

**[7]** *Massive geographische Datenvisualisierung mit WebGPU | TU Wien – Research Unit of
Computer Graphics.* https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/projects/weBIGeo
→ The host project.

**[8]** R. Preiner. 2011. *Screen-Space Triangulation for Interactive Point Rendering.*
Austrian-Russian Joint Seminar, Vienna.
https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2011/preiner11IR/
→ Rendering option, not implemented.

**[9]** Tong Wu, Zhiqiang Zhou, Anlan Wang, Yuning Gong, and Yanci Zhang. 2022. *A Real-Time
Adaptive Ray Marching Method for Particle-Based Fluid Surface Reconstruction.* Eurographics
Symposium on Rendering 2022.
→ Rendering option, not implemented. Fluid-optimised, so may look wrong for snow.

**[10]** Komon, Patrick & Kimmersdorfer, Gerald & Celarek, Adam & Waldner, Manuela. 2025.
*Data-Driven Compute Overlays for Interactive Geographic Simulation and Visualization.*
186–190. 10.1109/VIS60296.2025.00043.
→ The compute-node-graph architecture this plugs into, and the existing statistical
avalanche model the MPM solver sits **alongside**.

## Implementation references (not papers)

- **`mls-mpm88`** — Hu's ~88-line reference MLS-MPM implementation. The canonical source for
  the exact P2G/G2P factor placement, including the world-units/grid-units `dpos` asymmetry
  that is easy to get wrong.
- **weBIGeo docs** — `docs/webgpu_app_dev.md` (three-file recipe for adding a compute node),
  `docs/webgpu_engine.md` (Renderer vs Overlay, render pass order — needed for the 3D
  particle renderer), `docs/webgpu_base.md` (RAII wrappers, shader preprocessor).
- **`AVALANCHE_SIM_PLAN.md`** — the phased execution plan in the repo root.
- **`claude_log.md`** — session-by-session log of what was built and why.
